\title{Security-Aware Workload-State Counterfactual Verification and\\
Nodal Net-Value Settlement for Spatially Coupled AI Data Centers}
\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
AI data centers can provide demand response (DR), yet a meter decrease does not
show that the workload was committed before the event or that the change had
positive network value. This paper develops a declaration-indexed verifier for
spatially coupled data centers. Submit-time scheduler records define release,
runtime, GPU request, class, and region; a cumulative workload linear program,
a convex risk contract, and a contiguous runtime witness share this ledger and
its digest. An active-window energy map prevents carry-in declarations from
being omitted when the aggregate contract is scaled to locked days. The risk
fit enforces total and daily conditional value at risk (CVaR) limits together
with a predeclared validation-error non-inferiority certificate. For the 54-day
locked block, the causal Pareto contract reaches nRMSE 0.691, 1.895 MWh/day
false credit, 17.024 MWh/day under-credit, and $F_1=0.699$, weakly dominating
the declaration-causal workload-feasible comparator set (with strict gains over
the single feasible projection). The executable bridge enumerates 7,306,622
declaration-feasible starts, reproduces the optimum within
$2.3\times10^{-13}$ USD, and obtains total-profile nRMSE 0.036 while reporting
the flexible relative-L2 residual 1.410. A signed RTS-24 N--1 replay is kept
separate from the independent observed-meter scoring panel; it is not labeled
as a payable closed-meter settlement. The evidence therefore distinguishes
declaration feasibility, simulated mechanism isolation, and observational
scoring, and does not identify a causal utility treatment effect.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Index Terms---}AI data centers, counterfactual verification,
demand response, security-constrained dispatch, nodal settlement.

\section{Introduction}

The growth of large-model training and inference is converting data centers
from nearly static industrial loads into large, software-defined grid
resources. Interactive inference is latency constrained, elastic inference can
be delayed briefly, and batch GPU work can be deferred for hours or migrated
between regions. These capabilities enable
demand response (DR), but they also
invalidate the usual interpretation of a meter decrease. A reduction at one
facility can be followed by delayed consumption or simultaneous rebound at
another node. Paying the positive local decrease can therefore reward a
redistribution that creates little or negative network value.
Public traces already expose multi-week bursty workload arrivals and tens of
thousands of scheduler jobs, while facility telemetry reports GPU energy at a
different measurement boundary \cite{wang2024burstgpt,samsi2021supercloud}.

Conventional DR measurement and verification estimates the unobserved
no-event demand from historical meter data. Arithmetic historical baselines
remain common in market practice under published market guidance
\cite{caiso2017baseline}, despite known bias and manipulation incentives
\cite{wang2022baseline}. Regression-based facility models quantify
baseline uncertainty but do not encode a computing workload ledger
\cite{wang2022baseline}. More broadly, DR
research establishes the economic role of responsive load, yet a meter-only
estimator cannot determine whether queued jobs could actually have produced
its predicted trajectory.

Data-center scheduling studies provide this physical state: temporal deferral
and geographical balancing reduce energy cost and peak demand
\cite{cao2022flexibility}. Data-driven flexibility and
security-constrained coordination optimize spatial--temporal dispatch
\cite{cao2022flexibility,cao2024nonwire}. Balancing-market and load-shifting
studies provide complementary dispatch contexts. Virtual-link clearing and
space--time remuneration model geographical and temporal transfers
\cite{zhang2020virtuallinks,zhang2022remunerating}. Receding-horizon frequency
regulation retains multi-site state and future arrivals
\cite{zhang2023receding}. These
methods optimize operator-controlled schedules; verification of an independently
submitted DR counterfactual against an auditable workload ledger remains open.
Coupled-regulation models likewise provide no provenance-bound certificate
\cite{chen2021idccoupling}. Whole-facility measurements
also require a bottom-up GPU-to-facility conversion; calibration, benchmark
scaling, and network placement are therefore kept as separate evidence layers.

The second gap is electrical. Geographic workload control is often valued with
energy prices or gross megawatts, although nodal marginal value changes when
congestion or a contingency binds. Direct-current (DC) power flow and linear distribution
factors provide transparent sensitivities \cite{stott2009dc,zimmerman2011matpower};
N--1 dispatch imposes credible post-contingency limits through line-outage
distribution factors (LODFs) \cite{tejada2018lodf}. No data-center DR verifier
jointly certifies workload feasibility, signed spatial response, and
security-aware network value. Alternating-current (AC) feasibility is a separate
nonlinear check. Public traces support observational
replay but not a causal utility event, so measured execution, counterfactual
construction, and network valuation must remain separate.

This paper makes three power-system contributions. 1) It defines a typed
submit--execute--settle state in which a runtime-complete job witness is formed
from release, declared runtime, GPU request, and regional assignment. An
active-window energy map gives the exact declaration energy intersecting a
locked operating window, including carry-in jobs. The same indexed digest is
rechecked at regional aggregation and reused by the RTS-24 N--1 value replay.
2) It formulates a workload-state verifier with a causal information boundary,
a validation-selected high-5-of-10 Pareto projection, and a convex
total-plus-daily-CVaR contract. A triangle-inequality certificate constrains
the validation RMS error to a predeclared tolerance; in the locked fit it
selects the exact causal Pareto vertex while the epigraph controls absolute
false-credit exposure. The aggregate contract is
then mapped to a fixed linear dual-price surrogate and an exact finite
contiguous-block policy; primal target residuals remain explicit. 3) It gives
the workload response a signed nodal value under complete finite N--1
contingencies and separates declaration feasibility, network-value replay, and
meter-gated payment activation. This separation prevents a declaration
trajectory from being interpreted as a closed-meter payment. The resulting
coupling invariant carries one declared workload state into physical
verification and security-aware valuation; its LP, dispatch, and allocation
primitives retain established power-system semantics \cite{li2024computation}.
Because public traces do not identify utility geography, spatial conclusions
are conditional on the declared feature-stratified trace scenario and the
complete permutation/penetration panel. The certificate assumes trusted
scheduler provenance: a digest detects record changes but cannot authenticate
semantically false fields.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:model}
defines the spatial workload and network settlement problem. Section~\ref{sec:method}
presents the verifier, security-aware value rule, and allocation mechanism.
Section~\ref{sec:design} specifies the data, baselines, and locked protocol.
Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the core counterfactual, execution-replay,

Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes with the deployment boundary and future

\section{System Model and Problem Formulation}\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Spatial workload state}

Let $\mathcal S$, $\mathcal K$, $\mathcal D$, and $\mathcal T$ denote source
regions, workload classes, destination data centers, and 15-minute intervals;
the symbol $h$ is reserved for a calendar day in statistical panels.
The submit-time declaration ledger supplies arrival energy $a_{skt}$ for source
$s$, class $k$, and release interval $t$. The decision $x_{skd\tau}\geq0$ is the energy
served at destination $d$ in interval $\tau$. Its cumulative state is
\begin{equation}
 y_{skt}=\sum_{\tau=0}^{t}\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}x_{skd\tau}.
 \label{eq:cumulative}
\end{equation}
With cumulative arrivals $A_{skt}=\sum_{\tau=0}^{t}a_{sk\tau}$ and deadline
$D_k$, release and deadline feasibility are
\begin{align}
 y_{skt}&\leq A_{skt}, \label{eq:release}\\
 y_{skt}&\geq A_{sk,t-D_k},\quad t\geq D_k. \label{eq:deadline}
\end{align}
The state recursion is $y_{skt}-y_{sk,t-1}=\sum_d x_{skdt}$ with
$y_{sk,-1}=0$, and terminal service equals the declared arrivals due in the
window; the release and deadline inequalities are (\ref{eq:release}) and
(\ref{eq:deadline}), and the site-capacity constraint is
$\sum_{s,k}x_{skdt}\leq\bar P_d\dt$. Facility demand is
\begin{equation}
 p_{dt}=P_d^{\rm fix}+\dt^{-1}\sum_{s,k}x_{skdt}.
 \label{eq:power}
\end{equation}
For rolling window end $T^+$ and final event-day release $T^0$, the terminal
state is not forced to consume unexpired future work. Instead,
\begin{equation}
y_{skT^+}=A_{sk,\max\{T^0,T^+-D_k\}},
\label{eq:rollingterminal}
\end{equation}
which completes every job due inside the window and every event-day arrival
while retaining later real arrivals. The window starts $D_{\max}+96$ intervals
before and ends $D_{\max}$ intervals after the event; omitted earlier work has
expired. Equations (\ref{eq:release})--(\ref{eq:power}) map an aggregate preemptive
envelope to the facility meter. This is valid for checkpointable batch work;
Experiment~14 separately audits contiguous execution intervals, runtime, GPU
count, and native power.
For the counterfactual task-level check, let \(j\in\mathcal J_{\rm sub}\) index a
submitted scheduler record, \(r_j\) denote its submitted release, and let
\(d_j\) be the service-window endpoint. Slurm ``timelimit'' is an allocation run-time
declaration; it is converted to \(R_j=\lceil\mathrm{timelimit}_j/\dt\rceil\)
slots and combined with a precommitted queue allowance \(B=96\) slots, so
\(d_j=r_j+R_j+B\). The unlimited Slurm sentinel is mapped to the precommitted
horizon \(H_{\infty}=128\) slots before adding \(B\); finite declarations are
retained exactly. The $0.001$-MW-per-GPU nameplate is fixed before the split.
The training-calibrated entitlement \(\widehat E_j\) is retained only for the
aggregate Exp19 audit; the executable witness uses the declared runtime. Let
\(\bar p_{\rm GPU}=0.001\) MW/GPU be the committed nameplate, $s_j$ the
submit-time scenario region, and \(g_j^{\rm req}\) the requested GPU count.
Define the runtime-complete entitlement
\(E_j^{\rm run}=R_jg_j^{\rm req}\bar p_{\rm GPU}\dt\). The indexed service
variable \(u_{j\tau}\) is the energy assigned to job \(j\) in interval \(\tau\),
and the executable witness requires one contiguous fixed-rate block of exactly
\(R_j\) slots. Let \(w_{\rm batch}\) be the predeclared waiting-cost coefficient per
MWh-slot, and \(r^{\rm DR}\) the event service tariff. Define
\(c_j=g_j^{\rm req}\bar p_{\rm GPU}\dt\) and
\(\Theta_j=\{r_j,\ldots,d_j-R_j\}\). For each admissible start \(\theta\),
a binary \(z_{j\theta}\) selects the block and
\(u^{j\theta}_{\tau}=c_j\mathbf1\{\theta\leq\tau<\theta+R_j\}\). The exact
start-time mixed-integer linear program (MILP) is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 &\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_j}z_{j\theta}=1,\qquad z_{j\theta}\in\{0,1\},\\
 &u_{j\tau}=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_j}u^{j\theta}_{\tau}z_{j\theta},\qquad
   \sum_{j:s_j=d}u_{j\tau}\leq\bar P_d\dt,\\
 &\min_z\ \sum_{j,\theta\in\Theta_j}z_{j\theta}
   \sum_{\tau}\left[w_{\rm batch}(\tau-r_j)+r^{\rm DR}\mathbf1\{\tau\in\mathcal E\}\right]u^{j\theta}_{\tau}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:jobindexed}
\end{equation}
Every selected block enforces runtime completion, contiguity, and the GPU
nameplate; its positive regional slack makes the separable enumeration exact
for the displayed MILP. For delay
$\delta=\theta-r_j$, the waiting part of the objective is
$w_{\rm batch}c_j\{R_j\delta+R_j(R_j-1)/2\}$, so a long job is charged for every
delayed service interval rather than only for its start time. Experiment~27 enumerates every admissible start for all 75,326 records and
stores the earliest-start baseline and tariff response; Experiment~25 adds a
binding regional row. The measured
\([t_j^{\rm start},t_j^{\rm end}]\) interval is opened only for independent
post-event scoring after the declaration witness is fixed: 71,128 execution
matches never constrain \(z\), \(u\), or \(d_j\). Thus \(\widehat E_j\) is
calibration-only, \(E_j^{\rm run}\) is the nameplate-capacity commitment, and
the calibrated central energy is carried on the same blocks without being
presented as measured consumption. The risk target is linked through a fixed
regional slot price. Because the statistical and nameplate ledgers have
different scales, define the locked window set
$\mathcal W_{\rm lock}=\bigcup_{h\in\mathcal H_{\rm lock}}
\{96h,\ldots,96h+95\}$. The declaration energy active in this set is
\begin{equation}
 E_{\rm act}(\mathcal W_{\rm lock})=
 \sum_{j\in\mathcal J_{\rm sub}}E_j^{\rm dec}
 \frac{|[r_j,r_j+R_j)\cap\mathcal W_{\rm lock}|}{R_j},
 \label{eq:activewindow}
\end{equation}
where $E_j^{\rm dec}$ is either the nameplate upper entitlement or the
calibrated central entitlement. This overlap integral includes carry-in jobs
and uses only submit-time fields. Let
$\widetilde p^{\rm risk}_{dht}=P_d^{\rm fix}+\gamma_+[\bar p^{\rm risk}_{dht}-P_d^{\rm fix}]_+$,
with $\gamma_+=E_{\rm act}(\mathcal W_{\rm lock})/\sum_{d,h,t}
[\bar p^{\rm risk}_{dht}-P_d^{\rm fix}]_+\dt$. For locked day $h$, region
$d$, and interval $t$, define the fixed linear dual-price surrogate
$\pi_{dht}=r^{\rm DR}[\widetilde p^{\rm risk}_{dht}-P_d^{\rm fix}]_+ /
\widetilde\Pi_{\max}$, where $\widetilde\Pi_{\max}=\max_{d,h,t}
[\widetilde p^{\rm risk}_{dht}-P_d^{\rm fix}]_+$. Re-enumerating every
declaration-feasible block with the waiting, event-tariff, and $\pi_{dht}$
terms produces the exact minimizer of this finite linear-price objective. The
optimality statement concerns the declaration-feasible policy; equality to the
aggregate target is a separate primal residual. The bridge therefore records,
for every locked day,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 e^{\rm br}_{dht}&=p^{\rm exec}_{dht}-\widetilde p^{\rm risk}_{dht},\\
 \mathrm{nRMSE}_{\rm flex}&=
 \frac{\|e^{\rm br}\|_2}{\max\{\|\widetilde p^{\rm risk}-P^{\rm fix}\|_2,\epsilon\}}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:bridge_residual}
\end{equation}
along with the total-load normalization and the flexible relative-L2
denominator. Experiment~29 independently re-enumerates 7,306,622 candidate
starts, verifies the stored minimizer, and reports these residuals. The bridge
stores raw/scaled profiles, the common digest, daily residuals, and the
central-energy check; it is a deterministic realization of the fitted target
under the declared surrogate, not a second aggregate optimization.
For the job-to-network certificate, the indexed witness is mapped without a
second optimization. To avoid overloading the deadline endpoint $d_j$, let
$\mathcal J_{sk\delta}$ be the ledger partition whose source, class, and
declared destination labels are $s,k,\delta$; Experiment~27 uses the native
assignment $\delta=s_j$ and therefore has no outcome-dependent migration:
\begin{align}
 x^{\rm job}_{sk\delta\tau}&=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_{sk\delta}:\,r_j\leq\tau<d_j}
 u_{j\tau},\\
 p^{\rm job}_{\delta\tau}&=P_\delta^{\rm fix}+\dt^{-1}X^{\rm job}_{\delta\tau},\qquad
 X^{\rm job}_{\delta\tau}=\sum_{s,k}x^{\rm job}_{sk\delta\tau}.
 \label{eq:jobnetworkcoupling}
\end{align}
Aggregate migration remains a matched-workload control; (\ref{eq:jobnetworkcoupling})
is the indexed replay profile. Experiment~27 reconstructs $X^{\rm job}$ from
every stored $u_{j\tau}$ and verifies equality before network valuation; no
separately optimized aggregate receives a network value. Workload deferral and
geographic assignment follow
\cite{cao2022flexibility,cao2024nonwire}, while rolling-state control follows
\cite{zhang2023receding}; waiting and
migration costs are $w_k(\tau-t)x_{skd\tau}$ and $m_{sd}x_{skd\tau}$, with
service-time substitution differing only by a fixed-arrival constant.

The submit-time and post-event ledgers have separate canonical digests. Each
submitted record is represented by the scheduler-only tuple; its Secure Hash Algorithm 256 (SHA-256) digest is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \ell_j^{\rm sub}={}&(\mathrm{id}_j,t_j^{\rm submit},\mathrm{timelimit}_j,
 g_j^{\rm req},\kappa_j,\sigma_j),\\
 \mathcal L_{\rm sub}={}&\operatorname{sort}\{\ell_j^{\rm sub}:j\in\mathcal J_{\rm sub}\},\\
 h_{\rm sub}={}&\operatorname{SHA\text{-}256}\!\left(\operatorname{serialize}(\mathcal L_{\rm sub})\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ledgerdigest}
\end{equation}
where $g_j^{\rm req}$, $\kappa_j$, and $\sigma_j$ are the requested-GPU, class,
and scheduler-state fields. The resulting canonical commitment is
(\ref{eq:ledgerdigest}). Start/end times, DCGM energy, and telemetry counts
enter a separate execution ledger whose join and energy equality are checked
only after commitment. Canonical ordering makes $h_{\rm sub}$ independent of
execution telemetry \cite{nist2015fips1804}; Experiment~16 reconciles measured
power with the 118-MW nameplate fixed before the split.

\paragraph{Information and threat model.}
At decision time, $\mathcal I^{\rm dec}$ contains the submission ledger,
declared topology, pre-event prices, and configuration; the scoring set adds the
closed meter and execution ledger only after the contract is frozen. The threat
model covers post-commitment edits, replayed records, and attempts to introduce
future arrivals into a committed response. The canonical digest rejects an
altered scheduler tuple, the information-boundary certificate rejects future or
locked-test fields, and the settlement rule caps payment with the closed meter.
Scheduler and DCGM services remain trusted sources: a digest detects a changed
field, while upstream attestation is required to establish that a submitted
deadline, region, class, or energy field was semantically truthful. Complete-ledger
profiles are therefore ex-post information comparators; the slot-62 committed
ledger is the deployable contract protocol.

\subsection{Counterfactual, response, and false credit}

For event set $\mathcal E\subset\mathcal T$, $p$ denotes regional power in
MW and time sums use $\dt$ (MWh). The declaration-causal estimate is
$\widetilde p$; $p^0$ is the declaration-only strategic reference used for
mechanism isolation, $p^{1,\mathrm{sim}}$ is the independently solved
event-response schedule, and $p^{\rm obs}$ is the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) Data Center GPU Manager (DCGM) execution trace. The observed
trace is opened only after a profile is frozen; no utility event label exists,
so it is never a causal input. The mechanism-isolation response
$r=p^0-p^{1,\mathrm{sim}}$ is signed and retains rebound. For
$z\in\{\mathrm{obs},\mathrm{sim}\}$, submitted and true credit are
$\widehat r^{(z)}=[\widetilde p-p^{(z)}]_+$ and
$r^{(z)}=[p^0-p^{(z)}]_+$; false credit is
\begin{equation}
 F^{(z)}=\dt\sum_{d,t\in\mathcal E}
 [\widehat r^{(z)}_{dt}-r^{(z)}_{dt}]_+,
 \qquad z\in\{\mathrm{obs},\mathrm{sim}\}.
 \label{eq:falsecredit}
\end{equation}
The simulated source defines the validation epigraph; the observed source is
recomputed only in locked replay. False credit is computed from
(\ref{eq:falsecredit}) in absolute MW-slots, avoiding a ratio with a zero-credit
denominator. Before the event the operator freezes a contract baseline
$p^{\rm con}$. After meter closure, define the submitted gross reduction, the
contract-cap reduction, and the payable reduction by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
q^{\rm gross}_{dt}&=[\widetilde p_{dt}-p^{\rm obs}_{dt}]_+,\\
q^{\rm cap}_{dt}&=[p^{\rm con}_{dt}-p^{\rm obs}_{dt}]_+,\\
q^{\rm pay}_{dt}&=\min\{q^{\rm gross}_{dt},q^{\rm cap}_{dt}\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:meter_cap}
\end{equation}
The capacity payment is $Q^{\rm pay}=\dt\sum_{d,t\in\mathcal E}q^{\rm pay}_{dt}$.
Equation (\ref{eq:meter_cap}) uses only the submitted baseline, frozen contract,
and closed meter. Here $p^{\rm con}=p^{\rm safe}$ for the payment-band panel
and is the no-tariff committed-ledger profile for the decision-time panel;
$p^0$ remains an offline diagnostic. On locked event slots,
$\mathrm{nRMSE}=\sqrt{N_E^{-1}\sum_n(\widetilde p_n-p^0_n)^2}/
\max\{N_E^{-1}\sum_n p^0_n,\epsilon\}$. With
$M=\sum_n\min\{\widehat r_n,r_n\}$, precision, recall, and $F_1$ are
$M/\max\{\sum_n\widehat r_n,\epsilon\}$,
$M/\max\{\sum_n r_n,\epsilon\}$, and
$2PR/\max\{P+R,\epsilon\}$ over the $N_E$ region--slot samples.
The reported error metrics use mean absolute error (MAE) and root-mean-square error (RMSE); normalized RMSE (nRMSE) is the scale-free form above. The risk
simplex is the sole convex quadratic program (QP); all released workload
counterfactuals and payment caps use the linear programs stated above.

\subsection{Strategic reference scheduling}

Consider an arithmetic mean of $H=10$ reference days, event probability $q$,
and DR price $\pi^{\rm DR}$. If reference day $j$ adds event-window service
$\delta R_j$, the expected payment change is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\Pi=\frac{q\pi^{\rm DR}}{H}\sum_{j=1}^{H}\delta R_j
 -\sum_{j=1}^{H}\Delta C_j .
 \label{eq:profit}
\end{equation}
Let $c'_j$ be the right derivative of minimum operating cost with respect to
the event-window service constraint on day $j$. Linear-program sensitivity
\cite{boyd2004convex} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \frac{q\pi^{\rm DR}}{H}>\min_j c'_j
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad {}&
 \exists\ \text{a strictly profitable}\\[-2pt]
 &\text{local deviation}.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:threshold}
\end{equation}
At a degenerate optimum the current dual can be zero while the right marginal
cost is positive; Experiment~1 therefore checks $c'_j$ with independent
$5\times10^{-4}$ and $10^{-3}$ MWh finite differences (agreement tolerance
$10^{-6}$ USD/MWh). Equations (\ref{eq:profit})--(\ref{eq:threshold}) specialize
the historical-baseline premise in \cite{wang2022baseline}.

\subsection{Network value}

Let $M$ be the data-center-to-bus incidence matrix and
$p_t=p_t^{\rm native}+Mp_t^{\rm dc}$ the nodal demand vector. For
generator-to-bus map $C_g$, generator bounds, and power-transfer distribution
factor (PTDF) $H$ with line ratings $\bar f$, the
piecewise-linear security-constrained economic dispatch (SCED) value is
\begin{align}
 V(p)=\min_g\;&\sum_i C_i(g_i) \label{eq:sced}\\
 {\rm s.t.}\;&\mathbf1^\top g=\mathbf1^\top p,\quad
 \underline g\leq g\leq\bar g,\nonumber\\
 &-\bar f\leq H(C_gg-p)\leq\bar f.\nonumber
\end{align}
The incidence mapping aggregates demand at its connection bus; (\ref{eq:sced})
is the standard lossless DC representation \cite{stott2009dc,zimmerman2011matpower}.
The realized signed grid value is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta V=V(p^0)-V(p^1). \label{eq:value}
\end{equation}
Positive $\Delta V$ is a credit and negative $\Delta V$ a debit; no
positive-only truncation is applied.

\section{Proposed Methodology}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Model-based framework and matched-information pre-estimation}

The state, power, and network equations define the feasible state before a
payment target is formed (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}). Meter history and submitted
declarations enter the estimator in parallel; execution remains scoring-only.
Lagged meter/calendar baselines and declaration-view ledger comparators include
ridge, gradient boosting, and extremely randomized trees
\cite{hoerl1970ridge,friedman2001gradient}. Synthetic control and nonnegative
donor weighting are evaluated as a separate history-based class
\cite{geurts2006extratrees,abadie2010synthetic}.
The quantile output receives the same exact projection, with its penalty selected
by contiguous folds.

\subsection{Sparse cumulative workload-state verifier}

For statistical estimate $\widetilde p$ and each predeclared penalty $\rho_\ell$,
the first-stage program solves
\begin{equation}
 \min_{x,y,z}\ \mathcal C_{\rm w}(x)+\rho_\ell\sum_{d,t}z_{dt}
 \quad {\rm s.t.}\quad
 z_{dt}\geq\pm(p_{dt}(x)-\widetilde p_{dt}),
 \label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}
subject to (\ref{eq:cumulative})--(\ref{eq:power}). The absolute-value
epigraph is standard \cite{boyd2004convex}; its use for workload-ledger
verification is specific here. $\mathcal C_{\rm w}$ is workload cost, $C_i$ is
the generator-segment cost in (\ref{eq:sced}), and $V(p)$ is dispatch value, so
(\ref{eq:projection}) changes only the objective.
Six globally solved metadata schedules $p^{(\ell)}$ share identical arrivals
and constraints. A seventh causal Pareto projection is selected on validation
from the high-5-of-10 declaration target by a lexicographic rule over nRMSE,
false credit, under-credit, and $1-F_1$. The risk simplex contains these seven
causal profiles. A complete-ledger feasible-quantile projection is retained as
an external post-commitment comparator and is used only in the separate
payment hull. For a hull profile,
$\bar p=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\alpha_\ell p^{(\ell)}$, $\alpha\geq0$,
$\mathbf1^\top\alpha=1$, remains feasible because the common scheduling set is
convex.

The convex fit minimizes validation error plus total and daily-tail false-credit
exposure, measured by conditional value at risk (CVaR)
\cite{rockafellar2000cvar}. Let $\ell^\star$ minimize contiguous-fold nRMSE and
let $B_h$ be the absolute daily false-credit exposure of that single feasible
projection on validation day $h$. For a candidate simplex profile define
$F_h(\alpha)=\sum_{n\in h}f_n$, where $f_n$ is the positive-part false
credit at sample $n$. The risk fit uses absolute MW-slot exposure throughout;
there is no ratio with a potentially zero true-credit denominator. With
$m=\lceil(1-\gamma)H\rceil$ and reserve $\beta\in(0,1]$, solve
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\alpha,f,\nu,u}\quad
&\frac{\left\|\bar p-p^{0,\mathrm{decl}}\right\|_2^2}{N s_p^2}
 +\lambda\|\alpha\|_2^2
 +\omega_T\frac{\sum_{h=1}^{H}F_h}{S_T}\\[-2pt]
&\hspace{1.5em}+\omega_C\frac{\nu+m^{-1}\sum_{h=1}^{H}u_h}{S_C},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:riskobjective}
\end{equation}
Equation (\ref{eq:riskobjective}) fixes both preferences inside the fit. Here
$s_p^2=\max\{N^{-1}\|p^{0,\mathrm{decl}}\|_2^2,\epsilon\}$,
$S_T=\max\{\sum_hB_h,\epsilon\}$, and
$S_C=\max\{m^{-1}\sum_{h\in\operatorname{tail}_\gamma}B_h,\epsilon\}$.
Single-axis ablations remove one term and its constraint; the locked joint fit
uses $\omega_T=\omega_C=0.50$, $\gamma=0.75$, and $\lambda=10^{-8}$. The
budgets are
\begin{align}
 \sum_{h=1}^{H}F_h(\alpha)&\leq\beta\sum_{h=1}^{H}B_h,\label{eq:totalrisk}\\
 {\rm CVaR}_{0.75}\!\left(F_h(\alpha)\right)
 &\leq\beta{\rm CVaR}_{0.75}(B_h).\label{eq:cvar}
\end{align}
The positive part is represented without differentiating through a sort. For
each event sample $n$ and validation day $h$, introduce
$f_n\geq0$, a CVaR threshold $\nu\geq0$, and tail slacks $u_h\geq0$:
\begin{align}
 f_n&\geq p^\alpha_n-p^{(\mathrm{sim})}_n-r^{(\mathrm{sim})}_n,\\
F_h(\alpha)&=\sum_{n\in h}f_n,\\
 F_h(\alpha)-\nu-u_h&\leq0,\\
 \nu+\frac{1}{m}\sum_h u_h
 &\leq\beta\,\mathrm{CVaR}_{0.75}(B_h).
 \label{eq:riskepigraph}
\end{align}
The linear CVaR epigraph in (\ref{eq:riskepigraph}) uses the same $m$-day
denominator as the empirical statistic and avoids sorting. Here
$r^{(\mathrm{sim})}_n=[p^0_n-p^{(\mathrm{sim})}_n]_+$; the implementation uses
the equivalent threshold $p^{1,\mathrm{sim}}_n+r^{(\mathrm{sim})}_n$ and stores the
identity audit. To preserve the point-estimate metric, let
$q_\ell=N^{-1/2}\|p^{(\ell)}-p^{0,\mathrm{decl}}\|_2$ and
$q_\star$ denote the RMS error of the validation-selected causal Pareto
reference. The simplex additionally satisfies the linear
sufficient condition
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\alpha_\ell q_\ell
 \leq (1+\varepsilon_{\rm acc})q_\star,\qquad
 \varepsilon_{\rm acc}=10^{-6} .
 \label{eq:accuracycertificate}
\end{equation}
By the triangle inequality, (\ref{eq:accuracycertificate}) bounds the actual
simplex RMS error by the same right-hand side; it therefore prevents a risk
improvement from being purchased with an unreported forecast degradation.
Because the validation-selected causal Pareto profile is the minimum-RMS member
of this declared simplex, the zero-width certificate has an exact closed-form
optimizer: the one-hot causal Pareto vertex. This is the solution of the same
convex quadratic program, not a post-solution rule. Independent
Karush--Kuhn--Tucker stationarity and epigraph residuals are
recomputed after the solve; simplex, accuracy, and primal residuals are checked
as well. Four contiguous folds select
$\beta$ only when each training contract is feasible. A separately reported
payment-contract projection targets $\bar p$ while imposing
\begin{equation}
p^{\rm safe}_{dt}\leq p^{(\ell^\star)}_{dt}. \label{eq:envelope}
\end{equation}
The pointwise cap in (\ref{eq:envelope}) is the upper side of the contractual
credit band; it is evaluated on every event slot before settlement and is not
substituted for the scientific risk profile.
\paragraph{Two-sided credit band.}
To exclude a zero-credit envelope, tie a lower band to the fitted target. Define
$p^{\rm floor}_{dt}=\min\{p^{(\ell^\star)}_{dt},\bar p_{dt}\}$, where $\bar p$ is
the pre-projection validation fit. The event-slot constraints are
\begin{equation}
 p^{\rm floor}_{dt}-\varepsilon
 \leq p^{\rm safe}_{dt}\leq p^{(\ell^\star)}_{dt},
 \qquad (d,t)\in\mathcal D\times\mathcal E,
 \label{eq:twosided}
\end{equation}
The release sets $\varepsilon=0$; the lower side is therefore the exact
pointwise minimum of the two independently formed profiles, and the selected
single projection proves nonemptiness. Equations
(\ref{eq:totalrisk})--(\ref{eq:cvar}) control aggregate and daily-tail exposure,
while the lower side in (\ref{eq:twosided}) regularizes under-credit and gives
no unseen-trace guarantee.
Realized overpayment is handled by (\ref{eq:meter_cap}).

Reserved future jobs cannot be credited, and the tariff plan is scored against

To separate capacity planning from payment eligibility, the gate publishes a

 \widehat a^{\rm res}_{sk\tau}(d;\eta)=Q_{\eta}\!\left(\left\{a_{sk\tau}(h):h<d\right\}\right),

\label{eq:causalreserve}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Signed nodal and N--1 settlement}

Let $\lambda^0\in\partial V(p^0)$ be the nodal demand-value subgradient.
Signed linear settlement is $({\lambda^0})^\top(p^0-p^1)$, while
(\ref{eq:value}) is the realized signed system-value benchmark. Convexity
\cite{boyd2004convex} gives

\begin{equation}
 0\leq(\lambda^0)^\top(p^0-p^1)-\Delta V
 =V(p^1)-V(p^0)-(\lambda^0)^\top(p^1-p^0).
 \label{eq:bregman}
\end{equation}
Thus the linear rule upper-bounds exact value in the polyhedral market model;
equality requires a common supporting subgradient.

For market-compatible remuneration, let $\mathcal W$ contain the complete response cycle from anticipatory shift
through recovery. Its space--time remuneration is

\begin{equation}
 \Pi^{\rm ST}=\dt\sum_{t\in\mathcal W}\sum_d
 \lambda^0_{dt}(p^0_{dt}-p^1_{dt}).
 \label{eq:spacetime}
\end{equation}
This is the nodal virtual-link structure of
\cite{zhang2020virtuallinks,zhang2022remunerating}; summing site payments
reproduces (\ref{eq:spacetime}).

Space--time energy remuneration is one component of the contracted DR
product. Let $\mathcal D^{\rm part}$ be the predeclared participating sites,
$\mathcal E$ the event intervals, and $r^{\rm DR}$ the cleared service price.
The signed delivered capacity service and total operator value are
\begin{align}
 Q^{\rm cap}&=\dt\sum_{d\in\mathcal D^{\rm part}}\sum_{t\in\mathcal E}
 (p^\emptyset_{dt}-p^1_{dt}), \label{eq:capacityservice}\\
 G&=r^{\rm DR}Q^{\rm cap}+\Pi^{\rm ST}, \label{eq:operatorvalue}
\end{align}
where $p^\emptyset$ is the minimum-cost no-event schedule on the same window.
Equation~(\ref{eq:capacityservice}) defines the bilateral capacity product and
need not equal trace-anchored
$p^0$; $\Pi^{\rm ST}$ debits spatial transfer and recovery. This signed service
model follows demand-response mechanism design \cite{satchidanandan2023twostage,chen2021incentive}.

Let $x^1$ and $x^\emptyset$ induce $p^1$ and $p^\emptyset$. Define the
participant opportunity cost $C_{\mathrm{opp}}=\mathcal C_{\rm w}(x^1)-
\mathcal C_{\rm w}(x^\emptyset)$ and surplus $S=G-C_{\mathrm{opp}}$. With zero
disagreement utilities, the symmetric Nash contract \cite{nash1950bargaining} is

\begin{equation}
 z=\mathbb I\{S\geq0\},\quad
 P^{\rm B}=z\left(C_{\mathrm{opp}}+\frac{S}{2}\right),\quad
 u_{\rm dc}=u_{\rm op}=z\frac{S}{2}.
 \label{eq:nashcontract}
\end{equation}
Equation~(\ref{eq:operatorvalue}) combines the capacity payment and signed
space--time value. Equation~(\ref{eq:nashcontract}) uses nonactivation as the outside option,
keeps both utilities nonnegative, and is budget balanced. It is separate from
ISO nodal remuneration and the exact dispatch-value benchmark.
For the executable full-day ledger, each interval first retains the signed
contract value $s_{dt}=r^{\rm DR}q^{\rm pay}_{dt}+\Delta V_{dt}$, where power is
in MW, $\dt$ is in hours, and both terms are in USD. The reported economic
ledger is $S^{\rm signed}=\sum_{d,t}s_{dt}$; a cash presentation
$S^{\rm cash}=\sum_{d,t}\max\{s_{dt},0\}$ is stored separately. The cash floor
therefore leaves negative network-value debits visible in the signed cycle
total. The complete-cycle ledger $\mathcal W$ includes pre-event increases and
rebound, so workload conservation gives
$\sum_{d,t\in\mathcal W}(p^0_{dt}-p^1_{dt})\dt=0$ within solver tolerance.

Before payment comparison, the submitted, frozen-contract, and closed-meter
reductions are intersected as follows:
\begin{equation}
q^{\rm pay}_{dt}=\min\{q^{\rm obs}_{dt},q^{\rm cap}_{dt}\},\qquad
 G=r^{\rm DR}\!\sum_{d,t\in\mathcal E}q^{\rm pay}_{dt}+\Pi^{\rm ST}.
\label{eq:settlementchain}
\end{equation}
Equation (\ref{eq:settlementchain}) is evaluated after the submitted profile,
contract, and closed meter are fixed; the second line uses
$q^{\rm pay}$ in the corresponding ledger. Experiment~12 stores the observed
meter chain and its budget residual (below $10^{-8}$ USD). Experiment~27
instead stores the declaration replay
$q^{\rm replay}_{dt}=\min\{q^{\rm dec}_{dt},q^{\rm cap}_{dt}\}$ to certify
feasible network value. Exp30 records this role as declaration-only, and the
replay's signed/cash totals remain outside payable settlement until
$q^{\rm obs}$ is available.

Security-aware settlement augments (\ref{eq:sced}) with every finite
non-islanding single-line outage. If $L_{\ell k}$ is the LODF from outage $k$
to monitored line $\ell$, then
\begin{equation}
 -\bar f_\ell\leq
 (H_\ell+L_{\ell k}H_k)(C_gg-p)\leq\bar f_\ell,
 \quad \ell\neq k.
 \label{eq:n1}
\end{equation}
Equation (\ref{eq:n1}) is the standard preventive N--1 LODF formulation
\cite{tejada2018lodf}; every credible row uses native ratings. Islanding rows are
excluded because a connected single-reference PTDF cannot represent separate
islands, consistent with the DC/N--1 planning scope \cite{nerc2020tpl}.

\subsection{Scenario-robust N--1 payment certificate}

The pointwise envelope controls energy but not network payment because spatial
loads can activate different security constraints. Let $\mathcal P=\operatorname{conv}
\{p^{(1)},\ldots,p^{(L)}\}$ be the feasible-profile hull and
$p^{\rm cap}\in\mathcal P$ the validation-selected projection. Its penalty is
separate from the payment-target penalty; the feasible-quantile projection is an
external comparator. Let $\Xi_5$ be the 1st, 10th, 50th, 90th, and 99th
calibration-validation GPU-energy ratios from independent telemetry
\cite{samsi2021supercloud}. For facility profile $p^{\rm fac}_{dt}$, fixed demand
$P^{\rm fix}_d$, scale $s_{\rm dc}$, and factor $\xi$,

\begin{equation}
 p^{\rm dc,\xi}_{dt}=s_{\rm dc}\bigl(P^{\rm fix}_d+\xi[p^{\rm fac}_{dt}-P^{\rm fix}_d]\bigr),
 \qquad p^{\rm fac}_{dt}\geq P^{\rm fix}_d .
 \label{eq:fixedflexconversion}
\end{equation}
The scale uses the calibration-validation $q_{99}$ flexible peak, keeping the
fixed component invariant to $\xi$; raw endpoints remain activation scenarios.
The injection is $M p^{\rm dc,\xi}_t$, with superscripts $\alpha$ and ``cap''
applying (\ref{eq:fixedflexconversion}) to $p^\alpha$ and $p^{\rm cap}$. For
each settlement day, the \PaymentSafe{} defines
$B_\xi^{\rm cap}=\dt\sum_{t\in\mathcal E}
V_{N-1}(p_t^{\rm native}+M p_t^{\rm dc,\xi,cap})$ and solves
\begin{align}
\operatorname{lexmin}_{\alpha,e,\eta,\{g_t^\xi\}}\quad&
 \left(\sum_{d,t\in\mathcal E}e_{dt},-\eta\right)
 \label{eq:paymentcert}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\quad&
 p^\alpha=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\alpha_\ell p^{(\ell)},\nonumber\\
&\alpha\geq0,\quad\mathbf1^\top\alpha=1,\quad 0\leq\eta\leq1,\nonumber\\
&e_{dt}\geq\pm(p^\alpha_{dt}-p^{\rm safe}_{dt}),\nonumber\\
&g_t^\xi\ \text{satisfies (\ref{eq:sced}) and (\ref{eq:n1})},\nonumber\\[-2pt]
&\hspace{2em}\text{with }p_t=p_t^{\rm native}+M p_t^{\rm dc,\xi,\alpha},\nonumber\\[-2pt]
&\hspace{7.5em}t\in\mathcal E,\quad \xi\in\Xi_5,\nonumber\\
&\dt\sum_{t\in\mathcal E}\sum_iC_i(g_{it}^\xi)\nonumber\\[-2pt]
&\hspace{2em}\leq(1-\eta)B_\xi^{\rm cap},
\quad \xi\in\Xi_5.\nonumber
\end{align}
The piecewise-linear epigraph is solved by two global LPs: trajectory error is
minimized first, then the common fractional margin $\eta$. Feasibility is the
N--1 upper bound and $p^{\rm cap}$ proves nonemptiness. Hence, for any
$\xi\in\Xi_5$ and realized load $p^{1,\xi}$, daily payment obeys
\begin{multline}
\sum_{t\in\mathcal E}\dt
[V_{N-1}(p_t^{\rm native}+M p_t^{\rm dc,\xi,\alpha})
-V_{N-1}(p^{1,\xi}_t)]\\
\leq\sum_{t\in\mathcal E}\dt
[V_{N-1}(p_t^{\rm native}+M p_t^{\rm dc,\xi,cap})
-V_{N-1}(p^{1,\xi}_t)].
\label{eq:paymentdominance}
\end{multline}
The realized cost cancels, so this is a samplewise certificate without execution
truth. Every finite non-islanding contingency is imposed using standard SCED/LP
primitives \cite{zimmerman2011matpower,boyd2004convex}. The hull contains six
first-stage projections and the matched feasible-quantile comparator. \RiskSafe{}
is the validation-fitted simplex point under the two risk budgets; the separately
stored payment contract targets it under the pointwise cap and is excluded from
risk fitting.

The finite cap also has a set-valued certificate. Let
$p^{\rm cap}=p^{(\ell^\star)}$, $p^{\rm pay}$ be the separately selected
payment target, and $p^{\rm cert}=p^\alpha$. For
$\Delta V_\xi(p)=V_{N-1}(p,\xi)-V_{N-1}(p^{1,\xi})$ and
$p_\theta=(1-\theta)p^{\rm cap}+\theta p^{\rm cert}$, the interval is
 \begin{equation}
  \mathcal I_\xi=\left[
  \min_{0\leq\theta\leq1}\Delta V_\xi(p_\theta),\;
  \max\{\Delta V_\xi(p^{\rm cap}),\Delta V_\xi(p^{\rm cert})\}\right].
  \label{eq:paymentinterval}
 \end{equation}
The lower endpoint in (\ref{eq:paymentinterval}) is one joint N--1 SCED LP with
a shared segment parameter; convexity gives the endpoint maximum and the cap
belongs to $\mathcal I_\xi$.
Validation-frozen profiles and the realized counterfactual determine both
endpoints; the interval width is uncertainty cost, while (\ref{eq:paymentcert})
remains the finite-scenario certificate.

\subsection{Participant allocation}

For participants $N$, let $v(S)$ be signed dispatch value when coalition $S$
moves from baseline to actual demand. The exact allocation is
\begin{equation}
 \phi_i(v)=\sum_{S\subseteq N\setminus\{i\}}
 \frac{|S|!(|N|-|S|-1)!}{|N|!}
 [v(S\cup\{i\})-v(S)].
 \label{eq:shapley}
\end{equation}
This Shapley specialization in (\ref{eq:shapley}) uses signed grid value
\cite{shapley1953value};
coalition-size grouping gives $\sum_i\phi_i=v(N)$ when $v(\varnothing)=0$.
All 16 four-site and 256 eight-portfolio coalitions are enumerated exactly.
For larger contracts, exchangeable site slices retain binomial multiplicities and
reduce evaluation to $\prod_g(m_g+1)$ count states (tested through 20 participants).

\subsection{Analytical guarantees}

These certificates delimit the logical scope. Under the interior and
differentiability assumptions of (\ref{eq:profit}), the threshold in
(\ref{eq:threshold}) is exact; Experiment~1 checks all 56 cells. The workload
set, simplex schedules, and two-sided envelope are feasible. For any execution
$c$,
\begin{align*}
[p^{\rm safe}_{dt}-c_{dt}]_+&\leq[p^{(\ell^\star)}_{dt}-c_{dt}]_+,\\
[c_{dt}-p^{\rm safe}_{dt}]_+&\leq[c_{dt}-p^{\rm floor}_{dt}]_++\varepsilon .
\end{align*}
Summation gives the false-credit and under-credit bounds without locked outcomes.

Base-case and stacked N--1 values are convex piecewise-affine functions of nodal
demand; (\ref{eq:bregman}) gives the linear-value bound. Each dispatch embedded
in (\ref{eq:paymentcert}) is feasible, so (\ref{eq:paymentdominance}) holds for
the five frozen conversion scenarios $\Xi_5$. For the continuous audit in
Experiment~15, the realized counterfactual is held fixed while the affine
segment between the two frozen contract profiles is varied; the joint LP gives
the exact segment minimum and convexity puts its maximum at an endpoint. No
endpoint assertion is made for a simultaneously conversion-scaled realized
counterfactual. Experiments~9 and 15 use independent 40- and four-segment
models.

The digest detects post-commit scheduler-tuple changes, while the execution join
audits ordering and energy conservation. An optimum of (\ref{eq:jobindexed})
gives declaration-only runtime, capacity, and nameplate feasibility without
aggregate rounding. With $B_{dj}=\mathbf1\{s_j=d\}$ and bus map $M$, the typed
residuals are
\begin{align*}
r^{\rm job}_j&=\sum_{\tau=r_j}^{d_j-1}u_{j\tau}-E_j^{\rm run},\\
r^{\rm agg}_{d\tau}&=X_{d\tau}-\sum_jB_{dj}u_{j\tau},\\
r^{\rm map}_{b\tau}&=p_{b\tau}-P^{\rm fix}_{b\tau}-\dt^{-1}\sum_dM_{bd}X_{d\tau}.
\end{align*}
The first residual uses the same $E_j^{\rm run}$ as (\ref{eq:jobindexed});
$\widehat E_j$ remains diagnostic. Residuals at most $\varepsilon$ identify the
witness, checked in Experiments~22 and~27 before N--1 valuation; allocation
checks $\sum_i\phi_i=v(N)$ separately.
Moreover, if the feasible SCED value is $L_V$-Lipschitz on the committed
polytope, the mapped-value error is bounded by
\begin{equation}
 |V(p^{\rm map})-V(p^{\rm job})|
 \leq L_V\|r^{\rm map}\|_1 .
 \label{eq:typedbound}
\end{equation}
Equation~(\ref{eq:typedbound}) makes the zero-residual certificate explicit: the network solver receives
the same nodal profile implied by the indexed jobs, rather than an independently
refitted aggregate trace.

\section{Experimental Design}\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Data, preprocessing, and information boundary}

BurstGPT supplies 5,188,507 successful requests and token service
\cite{wang2024burstgpt}; MIT Supercloud supplies scheduler submissions and

75,326 valid submissions and 71,128 positive-energy execution matches, of which

\cite{samsi2021supercloud}. Submission time defines arrivals and measured

40 complete days. The GPU-power model uses 44,391 training jobs, 9,637
calibration-validation jobs, and 41,154 locked jobs; the calibration-validation
ratios define the five scenarios $\Xi_5$ in (\ref{eq:paymentcert}) without
synthetic replacement. Experiment~14 retains all 71,128 matches, whereas
Experiment~19 uses every valid declaration with a training-only aggregate
entitlement. The submit-time energy-label fit contains 71,141 positive
scheduler/DCGM pairs; 71,128 have valid measured intervals for replay, and the
13 label-only pairs cannot enter any decision. Experiment~27 forms the
runtime-complete declaration witness, while execution joins open only for
post-event scoring. The runtime window
plus a precommitted
96-slot queue allowance defines the contract window, not a
submit-to-completion interpretation of Slurm runtime, and membership is never
outcome-filtered.

Because the traces lack utility bus locations, four-region coupling is a
trace-driven benchmark: a predeclared feature-stratified round robin assigns
class, GPU count, runtime, and submission time. Experiment~11 exhausts all 24
permutations, three penetrations, and two concentration controls; Experiment~18
uses fixed generator-bus strata at native-load multiplier 0.90, so its transfer
result is conditional on that scenario. Calibration-validation ratios span
0.5827--5.3209 and the locked partition is a generalization audit. Equation
(\ref{eq:fixedflexconversion}) carries the fixed 6-MW/site component separately
and calibrates payment-network scale on $q_{99}$ at a predeclared 4\% peak
penetration; both raw endpoints remain activation-eligible under that N--1-feasible
scale. The separate flexible-only capacity-safe factor is retained as a planning
diagnostic and is not an endpoint clip. The Power Grid Library (PGLib) and
MATPOWER/PYPOWER provide topology, ratings, and costs
\cite{babaeinejadsarookolaee2021pglib,zimmerman2011matpower}; RTS-24 follows
the published case \cite{grigg1999rts}.

\subsection{Baselines, parameters, and inference}

Experiment~2 compares meter/statistical baselines with complete-ledger
quantile/feasible projections, synthetic control, \TailRisk, the selected
single projection, and \RiskSafe; \PaymentSafe{} is evaluated separately in
Experiments~9 and 15. Feature equality is audited while execution truth is

$F_1$ throughout the locked scoring tables.
Moving
three-day bootstrap replicates and all $2^{18}$ block-sign assignments use Holm correction
\cite{kunsch1989bootstrap,holm1979multiple}. Sparse-system size grows from

(0.039--0.212 s).

profile-validated checkpoint before a resumed cell is accepted.

Experiment~30 audits active-window scaling, information-set lineage, and the
closed-meter payment gate.
Comparisons use the same 54 locked days unless a deterministic
network or ledger rule is stated. Experiment~26 independently recomputes the
declaration-to-network residuals and checks the common witness digest, source
hashes, and role labels of the risk, payment, and outage artifacts.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Incentive boundary and independent verification}

Experiment 1 solves the entire $8\times7$ DR-price/call-probability grid.

Experiment~2 evaluates twelve methods on the locked 54-day panel
(Table~\ref{tab:base}). The causal Pareto risk contract reaches
nRMSE/$F_1$/false credit/under-credit of 0.691/0.699/1.895/17.024 MWh/day.
It strictly improves all four locked metrics over the single feasible projection
(0.943/0.463/2.470/26.812) and weakly dominates every other declaration-causal,
workload-feasible comparator in the table. The selected profile is the exact
one-hot causal Pareto vertex returned by the joint convex program; this is
expected because the $10^{-6}$ RMS certificate makes that validation-selected
vertex the unique admissible minimum-RMS solution. The validation certificate
records reference and fitted MSE 188.931512 versus an upper bound of
188.931701, an RMS bound of 13.745236 against 13.745243, zero KKT and primal

admissible declaration-time comparator. Total-only, CVaR-only, and joint
ablations return the same certified vertex, so the two contractual axes are

replay remains separate: it supplies observational scoring and never enters
fitting or reserve selection.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper couples a provenance-bound workload verifier with signed nodal
valuation. The locked causal Pareto contract reaches
nRMSE/false credit/$F_1$=0.691/1.895/0.699 while satisfying its predeclared
total and daily-tail budgets and weakly dominating the declaration-causal
workload-feasible comparison set (with strict gains over the single feasible
projection; Fig.~\ref{fig:result_certificates}).
The witness covers 75,326 submissions,
5,184 RTS-24 cells, and 655.753 MWh of active declaration energy. The exact
start audit reports total nRMSE 0.03631 and flexible relative-L2 residual 1.410.
The signed network replay retains negative cells and is reported separately
from the independent meter score; it is a feasibility/value certificate rather
than a closed-meter payment. Deployment requires scheduler, telemetry, and
topology attestation, together with a payment gate that opens only after the
closed meter is available.
